# ChipForge, Bittensor subnet 108: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of ChipForge. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 27, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 108. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "ChipForge decentralizes silicon design. Miners anywhere compete to build chips that accelerate AI, scored on real AI models."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $9.43 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $9.03 and as high as $14.02, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 10 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, all 10 are still in the contest and 1 already has its fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

## The contest today

210 of the 256 slots are taken, so a new entry does not remove anyone yet, and an entry cannot be removed for about 17 hours after it is made. Nobody outside the contest's owner is being paid today.

## How the entries from two to four weeks ago ended up

30 entries from 22 wallets entered between Nov 1 and Nov 12, so each of them has had two full weeks, and they paid a typical fee of $11.29. After 7 days none of them were being paid $1 a day or more, and after 14 days none were. 3 of them, which is 10%, have by now been paid at least their fee, and the typical entry ended $11.22 behind its fee before any cost of running it. By week of entry, oldest first, the share that got the fee back was 14% and 0%, so it has been falling from week to week.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A ChipForge miner downloads a chip specification and testbench, writes the circuit in Verilog or SystemVerilog, and submits a ZIP with a signature that covers the ZIP’s file hash. The signature comes from the hotkey, the key a miner works with, and the wallet hotkey must be registered on subnet 108. The miner service runs continuously on a public port, which is 8091 by default. The miner prerequisites list no hardware minimums, paid account or separate API key, and the CLI guide lists no upload or submission fee.

Validators are participants that score the miners’ work, and they check correctness against the testbench. The README lists speed, chip area and estimated power as scoring metrics, and the v2 validator reports the evaluator’s overall score S for a passing design and zero for a rejected design. To set a record, the best qualifying design in a batch must beat the standing score by any margin the challenge requires.

The code pays recent records after probation while their payout windows are open. With three eligible records on the default ladder, the newest gets about 51%, the next gets 30.6% and the oldest gets 18.4% of the miner pool. That pool defaults to 10% of validator weight unless the server overrides it, and the rest is burned.

The ZIP limit is 50 MB, and the README lists five attempts per hotkey, subject to the challenge settings. The CLI shows the time remaining when the server supplies a challenge deadline, and the miner guides reviewed give no standard round length or post-registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/TatsuProject/ChipForge_SN108 at commit 91cc393 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
