## What mining here takes, read from its code

Miners fetch assigned web pages and upload their text, or they run Desearch’s model and upload one vector per text. Either job can run on its own with no public port and a registered Bittensor hotkey, the key a miner works with.

Crawling can run on a CPU, and the hardware guide lists two cores, 8 GB of RAM and 48 GB of free storage. Embedding needs an NVIDIA GPU with at least 16 GB of memory, preferably 24 GB. The crawl guide calls for proxies, but the code supports direct connections and ScrapingDog is optional.

Validators, the participants who score the miners’ work, fetch sample pages again and recompute sample vectors. Miners divide 20% of emissions by credited pages and 50% by credited text characters over the last 24 hours, while 30% and any unearned pool are burned. Unchecked uploads earn at the miner’s checked rate over the preceding 72 hours.

Crawl uploads are due in three minutes and vector uploads in five, with ten seconds of upload grace. Every upload is checked until the hotkey passes ten checks for that job, and two failures among its last ten checks erase a day’s credit and block that job for 48 hours. Registration protection, minimum uptime and additional fee amounts are not stated. Read from https://github.com/datura-ai/desearch at commit ff5ce35 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
