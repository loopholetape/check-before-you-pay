# Carbon, Bittensor subnet 116: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Carbon. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 26, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 116. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Transforming engineering simulations through machine learning"

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $1.53 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $0.10 and as high as $35.96, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 707 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 382 are still in the contest and 1 already has its fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Carbon miner builds a fast model of electric-motor torque, tests it locally and submits a recipe that the validator, the participant that scores the miners’ work, rebuilds. Motor 1.8 is the only paying challenge on subnet 116.

CPU practice needs no wallet or model key, and the supported setup uses Ubuntu, Docker, four CPUs, 32 GB of RAM and about 150 GiB of free disk. To submit, you need a registered hotkey, the key a miner works with, and the signer must stay running until reconstruction. Optional model calls and GPU rental cost extra, and Carbon recorded a $5 submission fee, but I found no confirmation that it collects it.

Models must pass physics checks before hidden tests give equal weight to torque accuracy and the quality of the model’s top five designs. The official score must beat the leader by more than 0.0129, or exceed 0.0629 for an empty crown. Motor’s leader earns 25% of miner emissions for 24 hours, and the rate halves daily until another qualifying improvement, while the remainder burns.

Each round lasts about 72 minutes and allows one graded attempt per group per challenge, and a lead gained and lost between updates earns nothing. I found no post-registration protection period in the cited mining guide or grading rules. Read from https://github.com/carbonphysicsai/Carbon at commit 4eb6d8c on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
