# Fugal, Bittensor subnet 70: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Fugal. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 27, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 70. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Open competition to train cost-aware LLM routers, with hardware-attested benchmarks."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $0.09 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $0.09 and as high as $52.09, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 147 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, all 147 are still in the contest and 4 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 17 hours after an entry is made. $608 a day is paid to entrants today, 1 wallet receives $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 100% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A miner on Fugal trains a router to predict which language model will answer a question correctly for its cost, then commits the router head’s hash on chain and serves proof of paid model calls. Mining uses a Bittensor hotkey, which is the key a miner works with, along with a funded OpenRouter key and an approved Google Cloud C3 confidential VM running dstack. The backbone computes embeddings on CPU in float32, and the dependencies and backbone need about 4 GB of disk. A validator is a participant that scores the miners’ work, and validators reach the miner on port 8091.

Fugal estimates routine v4 calls and hosting at $20.60 a day, compared with about $119 for a v3 challenger. Using measured v3 call costs, Fugal estimates that a 500-question challenge costs an additional $16 and that a 1,000-question challenge costs an additional $32.

Before block 9,572,760, v3 judges predictions and checks the value of model choices. From that block, taking the seat under v4 requires better predictions over at least 300 fresh outcomes and a paid challenge, and against a holder, the challenge must show that the router’s choices hold up after costs. Eligible champions receive 85%, 10% and 5% in succession order, newest first. A former champion’s unpaid share goes to the current champion if eligible, and any remainder burns.

Rounds last about 72 minutes, with proofs due halfway through. The launch guide gives an immunity end block for the subnet but gives no protection period for an individual miner. Calls in flight can take the bill above the default $100 ceiling. Read from https://github.com/fugal-ai/fugal-subnet at commit 5176fa7 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
