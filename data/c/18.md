## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Zeus miner forecasts hourly temperature, both wind components and incoming sunlight worldwide for two and fifteen days. Rounds start at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 UTC, and miners commit a hash between minutes 30 and 45.

To serve forecasts, a miner needs a public port, four 2.5 GHz CPU cores, 16 GB of RAM, 4 GB of swap and 10 GB of free storage. A registered hotkey, the key a miner works with, is required, but no GPU, third-party key or additional fee is specified for serving.

Validators, who are participants that score the miners' work, later request the exact compressed bytes and compare the forecast with ERA5 once the full period is available. They average absolute and root mean squared errors, giving extra weight to Germany and the rest of Europe. For each variable and horizon, wins over the last four short or eight long results determine who gets 95% of the weight, while other eligible miners share 5% by rank. Long forecasts carry 80% before the mainnet burn, and miners with incomplete histories rank last but can receive weight.

The example generates random numbers. New miners get four weeks of grace from exclusion after eight consecutive penalties on a challenge, but the repository gives no deregistration immunity period. Read from https://github.com/Orpheus-AI/Zeus at commit f4d7826 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
