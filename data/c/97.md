# Ingot, Bittensor subnet 97: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Ingot. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 28, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 97. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Open models, cast small."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $10.11 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $9.03 and as high as $37.41, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 51 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, all 51 are still in the contest and 8 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 17 hours after an entry is made. $2,237 a day is paid to entrants today, 2 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 50% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

Miners on Ingot, Bittensor subnet 97, change the weights of a compressed Qwen3.8-27B model and privately submit one Q2 GGUF file capped at 7,206,168,928 bytes. A miner uses a Bittensor coldkey, the key that holds the funds, and a hotkey, the key a miner works with, and the subnet supplies upload credentials. The training example uses eight GPUs, but the guide specifies no minimum GPU memory, disk capacity, public port or always-on server, and it lists no mandatory paid service or extra fee.

The validator, a participant that scores the miners' work, compares next-token probabilities on 800 prompts in about 45 minutes. A challenger normally wins by reducing the current winner’s error by at least 2%. Each of the five newest winners gets 20% for up to five days, but that ends sooner if the winner is displaced or its UID changes hands, and the remainder burns at UID 0.

Upload credentials last one hour, and the code starts the 24-hour deadline to mark the upload ready when credentials are issued. The guide gives no registration protection period or fixed round length. An ordinary validation failure allows a retry within three upload attempts, but an evaluation or duplicate rejection uses up the hotkey. The guide describes similarity checks, while the active Q2 code checks for identical weight files. Read from https://github.com/DendriteLabs/ingot at commit 28e16f3 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
