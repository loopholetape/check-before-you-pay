# SOMA, Bittensor subnet 114: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of SOMA. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 26, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 114. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Context compression layer delivered through MCP infrastructure"

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $91.31 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $45.94 and as high as $114, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 100 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 80 are still in the contest and none has its fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid $0.12 a day today, up from $0.00 a week ago, so at today's pay the fee would take about 752 days to come back, provided the entry lasts that long.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 17 hours after an entry is made. $6,659 a day is paid to entrants today, 5 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 40% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A SOMA miner uploads a Python script that shortens a coding agent’s conversation history while preserving the information the agent needs to fix software issues. Validators, the participants who score the miners’ work, run the script, so the guide lists no miner hardware minimum, uptime requirement or public port. You need a hotkey, the key a miner works with, registered on subnet 114, and you need an OpenRouter key to pay for your evaluation calls. Baseline calls use SOMA’s key, and DeepSeek V4 Pro rounds require the DeepSeek provider and both data-collection settings.

Validators compare test passes and weighted token use. On standard tasks, 80% of baseline successes earns full token credit, while 50% or less earns only a penalty. After any burn, the best score across all three task lengths earns 25%, each pair earns 15%, and each length earns 10%, with ties split equally.

The upload code allows one script per hotkey per round, and the general guide says `main()` and tells miners to check the entry point for their competition. Edits must preserve protected next steps and the meaning of what remains without introducing instructions. The miner setup guide gives no registration protection period, and the OpenRouter guide quotes no fixed evaluation price or separate upload fee. Read from https://github.com/DendriteHQ/SOMA at commit 611a7b2 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
