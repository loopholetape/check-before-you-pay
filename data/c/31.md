# rec4ll, Bittensor subnet 31: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of rec4ll. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 22, 2026 at 08:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Recall is a decentralized RAG and memory API on Bittensor. Miners serve embeddings, reranking, vector search and LLM generation. The Recall API checks their work with continuous verified probes and real customer traffic, and validators set weights from those scores. Developers get encrypted vector databases, search and a free memory MCP for AI coding apps through one simple API."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $74.98 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $45.16 and as high as $534, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 229 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 161 are still in the contest and 28 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid $12.41 a day today, up from $0.00 a week ago, so at today's pay the fee would take about 6 days to come back, provided the entry lasts that long.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 17 hours after an entry is made. $2,823 a day is paid to entrants today, 231 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes under 1% of the total.

## How the entries from two to four weeks ago ended up

8 entries from 8 wallets entered between Nov 4 and Nov 8, so each of them has had two full weeks, and they paid a typical fee of $239. After 7 days none of them were being paid $1 a day or more, and after 14 days 6 were. None of them has been paid as much as its fee, and the typical entry ended $152 behind its fee before any cost of running it. A group of 8 is small, so these shares can swing a long way on a few entries.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. This page also cannot say what the contest asks of an entry, which hardware it needs or how it decides who is paid, and for that the contest's own code and rules have to be read. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.

## A pack for this contest, which is not for sale yet

This page cannot tell you what rec4ll asks of an entry, so we read its code and wrote a pack for it. A pack holds two things: an account of what the contest really pays for, in which every statement about scoring names the file of the contest's code it was read from, and a practice judge that your agent runs on your own machine against your own entry, so that it sees roughly what the entry would score before the fee is paid.

For rec4ll the pack holds the following.

- An account of how passes become pay, read from the contest's public code: the eight checks and their pass marks, the stages a new seat goes through and the allocation arithmetic, with the file and function named for every statement.
- What the chain shows against that code: the pay is an almost equal split among the seats that clear the marks, answer generation is 30% of a full share although the miner's guide calls it optional, and only one hotkey for each coldkey is paid.
- Where entries lose: the hours a new seat needs before it is paid in full, set against the protected period, the strict marks of the vector store, and the failures that send a seat back to no pay.
- A practice judge that repeats the contest's pay arithmetic exactly, shows when a new seat is first paid and fully paid, and sends stand-in probes to your own embedding, rerank, vector store and generation services before you register.

To use its practice judge you need the following.

- Python 3.9 or newer and nothing else for pay, ladder, proof and selftest.
- For backends, your own miner stack running from the contest's Docker Compose files, because the services are reachable only inside the Compose network; the judge is run inside one of the stack's own containers, which already hold Python.
- For backends with the vector store, the stack's own internal key, passed through an environment variable or a file and never on the command line.
- For backends with generation, your own account at the generation provider, since every generation probe is a paid request.
- For door, a second machine outside your network for the public side, and the certificate file copied from the miner.

The pack was written on 2026-11-22 from rec4ll-main/recall-validator at commit 3c05a47 and rec4ll-main/recall-miner at commit 876b489. It says plainly what it cannot tell you, which is the following.

- The contest's probes and its rules for a pass are private, so no pass rate measured by the practice judge is the pass rate the contest will measure.
- The practice judge has been run against stand-in services only and never against a live miner stack.
- How fast is fast enough, how much customer work is credited and how long it takes from paying to the first probe are not public.

There are three things about the seller that a buyer has a right to know. We are not entered in rec4ll ourselves, though we do enter other contests on Bittensor. The practice judge is our own program, built from the contest's public code and rules, and it is not the contest's own judge, so a pass there is not the contest's verdict. If the pack turns out not to be what this page says, ask for your money back within seven days of paying, in the way the page on how to buy describes, and it is sent back to the wallet that paid.

It is not for sale yet, because its practice judge has only met stand-ins so far, and it goes on sale once it carries numbers from a seat that is really running. It would cost $35, by one rule that holds for every pack: about half of today's entry fee, never under $20 and never over $60 (the fee here is $74.98 today). Until then there is nothing to pay and nothing to fetch, and this section only says that the pack exists.
