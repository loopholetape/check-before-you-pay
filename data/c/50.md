In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 50. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $146 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $145 and as high as $237, because it moves with demand. Here 50% of that price is held as collateral on the entry and is released only as the entry earns, while the rest is burned when the entry is made, so an entry that is never paid loses all of it. This page counts the whole price as the fee and counts collateral as coming back once it is released.

Of the 76 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 69 are still in the contest and none has its fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid $2.67 a day today, up from $1.54 a week ago, so at today's pay the fee would take about 55 days to come back, provided the entry lasts that long.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Synth miner sends 1,000 possible price paths per regular request to validators, the participants who score miners’ work. The paths cover one or 24 hours for crypto and 24 hours for equities and commodities, and the changelog gives mainnet intervals of 1, 12 and 144 minutes per asset, respectively.

The reference miner needs two 2.5 GHz CPU cores, 16 GB RAM, 12 GB swap and 10 GB free disk space, and an SSD is preferred. It can run without a GPU, but it must stay reachable on TCP port 8091. The guide calls for a registered Bittensor hotkey, the key a miner works with, and a wallet funded with at least 0.25 TAO for registration. Cloud hosting is optional, the reference miner fetches prices without a paid key, and the setup guide lists no additional fee.

Validators compare forecast price moves with actual moves, including volatility for one-hour crypto, and lower five- or ten-day average scores earn larger shares. The code assigns 22% to the owner burn and splits the rest across three competitions, or four when the 10-second Synth Ultra scores are included.

Replies are due by the forecast start time, which can leave less than a minute for one-hour crypto. When a prompt has valid scores, missed replies receive a 95th-percentile penalty despite the README’s 90th-percentile figure, and earlier requests in a newcomer’s scoring window can carry that penalty too. The registration instructions give no duration for protection from deregistration. Read from https://github.com/mode-network/synth-subnet at commit 8e91962 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
