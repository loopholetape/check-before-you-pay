# Swarm, Bittensor subnet 124: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Swarm. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 28, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 124. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $91.59 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $90.32 and as high as $146, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 59 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, all 59 are still in the contest and 8 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 17 hours after an entry is made. $3,840 a day is paid to entrants today, 6 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 33% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Swarm miner submits software that flies a simulated drone, reports people and returns to its dock. Swarm lists three 2.5 GHz CPU cores, 8 GB of RAM, 4 GB of swap and 20 GB of free storage. A GPU is optional, and the miner can go offline once the upload signed by its registered Bittensor hotkey, the key a miner works with, succeeds. The submission code mentions a transaction fee but gives no amount or separate upload charge, and the miner instructions specify no third-party account or public miner port.

Detection counts for 70%, the area searched for 20% and flight for 10%, though a disqualifying flight can score zero. When nobody is present on a run, the detection score is the share of the area searched divided by one plus the number of false alarms. Each miss or false alarm zeroes five of the best runs before averaging, and coming within one metre of the Test Field boundary or leaving the field zeroes that run and counts as one more mistake under the same rule.

Sentinel has 95% of emissions and a window of ten champions, while Test Field has 5% and a window of three. The validator, a participant that scores the miners' work, skips administratively dropped champions before filling those places. The guide calls shares frozen at crowning, but the validator recalculates them as ranks change. Each older rank gets 70% of the preceding base weight, and the improvement that earned the win can add up to 30% to that weight.

Each hotkey gets one model in one competition, even if evaluation fails. Rounds have lasted 14 days since epoch 19, and new commitments close 90 minutes before rollover. The upload window is six hours, though the same file can arrive later. The miner guide does not specify a post-registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/swarm-subnet/swarm at commit c5642c2 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
