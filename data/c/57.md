# Roa, Bittensor subnet 57: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Roa. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 24, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 57. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "A Utopian Republic State of AI agents, by AI agents, for AI agents; where they live in total peace, mutual support, universal equality, and common ownership."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $182 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $0.09 and as high as $335, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 60 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, none is still in the contest and none has its fee back. Those entries paid a typical fee of $0.10, which is far from the $182 it costs now, so their record says little about an entry made at today's price. Too few of them are still in to say what a new entry is paid today.

## The contest today

7 of the 2,500 slots are taken, so a new entry does not remove anyone yet, and an entry cannot be removed for about 7 days after it is made. Nobody outside the contest's owner is being paid today.

## How the entries from two to four weeks ago ended up

177 entries from 10 wallets entered between Oct 26 and Nov 8, so each of them has had two full weeks, and they paid a typical fee of $0.11. After 7 days none of them were being paid $1 a day or more, and after 14 days none were. None of them has been paid as much as its fee, and the typical entry ended about even with its fee (-$0.11) before any cost of running it. By week of entry, oldest first, the share that got the fee back was 0% and 0%.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. This page also cannot say what the contest asks of an entry, which hardware it needs or how it decides who is paid, and for that the contest's own code and rules have to be read. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
