# ConnitoAI, Bittensor subnet 102: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of ConnitoAI. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 24, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 102. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Contributors train specialized expert modules that are aggregated into powerful AI systems, without massive centralized compute."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $16.37 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $0.23 and as high as $40.37, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 726 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 125 are still in the contest and 41 already have their fee back. Those entries paid a typical fee of $3.05, which is far from the $16.37 it costs now, so their record says little about an entry made at today's price. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 17 hours after an entry is made. $1,549 a day is paid to entrants today, 1 wallet receives $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 100% of the total.

## How the entries from two to four weeks ago ended up

945 entries from 40 wallets entered between Oct 25 and Nov 10, so each of them has had two full weeks, and they paid a typical fee of $11.28. After 7 days none of them were being paid $1 a day or more, and after 14 days none were. 77 of them, which is 8%, have by now been paid at least their fee, and the typical entry ended $9.70 behind its fee before any cost of running it. By week of entry, oldest first, the share that got the fee back was 8%, 11% and 6%.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A miner trains a small add-on for a fixed Qwen language model, uploads it to Hugging Face and records its version on Bittensor. The miner FAQ calls for one 48 GB NVIDIA GPU, about 62 GB of system RAM or more and about 100 GB of free disk space. You need a registered Bittensor hotkey, the key a miner works with, and a Hugging Face repository that validators, the participants who score the miners' work, can read. Your token needs write access to submit.

Training scores reward better predictions and penalize damage to general-text performance, and miners with exactly tied training scores receive zero rank points. By default, the code reserves 25% of the weight for the owner. When both training groups are filled, up to three eligible miners share 73.5% and up to five others share 1.5% according to their average rank points. The first group requires scores in at least three of the last five cycles, and if nobody qualifies, its share goes to the owner. The code defaults prompt weighting to off and counts 13 training cycles per prompt round.

If the upload fails, you miss that training round. The owner’s service sets the training phase boundaries by block height, and the migration plan mentions a post-registration protection period without giving its length. Read from https://github.com/Connito-AI/Connito at commit f54dda5 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
