# Attune, Bittensor subnet 58: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Attune. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 25, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 58. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "The open frontier for adaptive robot intelligence"

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $8.55 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $8.55 and as high as $85.36, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 50 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, all 50 are still in the contest and 5 already have their fee back. Those entries paid a typical fee of $58.87, which is far from the $8.55 it costs now, so their record says little about an entry made at today's price. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

Miners on Attune submit robot models to Vector or collect training data for Horizon, where contributors control simulated robots or make recordings. A Vector miner uses a write token to upload trained weights to Hugging Face and records the revision and file hash on chain. Vector charges no submission fee.

The validator, a participant that scores the miners’ work, runs both models on its GPU, showing each one demonstration before testing it in a new scene. Across 15 trials on each of 16 tasks, the challenger must beat the champion’s average success rate by at least three percentage points. The four latest champions split Vector’s 30% of miner rewards in shares of 40%, 30%, 20% and 10%, newest first. On Horizon, one week’s accepted work sets the next week’s score, and each registered Horizon hotkey, the key a miner works with, receives its score as a share of total miner rewards.

On Vector, once the lane’s configured start block is reached, the first commitment from a registration spends its only entry, and swapping hotkeys cannot restore it. Any remainder of Horizon’s 70% and any share assigned to an unregistered hotkey burn. The pinned README gives no post-registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/robotensor/attune-subnet at commit 40a4575 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
