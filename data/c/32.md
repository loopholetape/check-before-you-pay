# ItsAI, Bittensor subnet 32: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of ItsAI. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 27, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 32. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "ItsAI is a bittensor subnet focused on high-quality AI detection for texts. Recognised as the most accurate AI detector by MGTD benchmark."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $9.17 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $2.07 and as high as $16.67, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 719 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 139 are still in the contest and 501 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid $5.72 a day today, about the same as a week ago, when it was $5.83, but most entries here do not last: only 139 of those 719 are still in, and an entry that is removed keeps what it was paid and loses its fee, so the days it would take to earn the fee back at that pay mean little.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 24 hours after an entry is made. $1,275 a day is paid to entrants today, 78 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 32% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A miner on It's AI, Bittensor subnet 32, reads passages from validators, which are participants that score the miners' work, and estimates the probability that AI wrote each word. The supplied miner repeats one estimate across each passage.

The hardware specification lists a 24 GB GPU, four 2.5 GHz CPU cores, 24 GB RAM and 20 GB of free storage, although the mining guide suggests a 16 GB RTX A4000. The miner runs continuously and needs a public port because validators contact it directly. Registration requires a Bittensor wallet and a hotkey, which is the key a miner works with, but the default alpha stake is zero and the mining guide lists no third-party miner credentials or extra fees.

The validator requests 30 human samples and 90 AI samples despite the guide’s equal split. On in-domain words, it averages F1, average precision and one minus the fraction of those words falsely flagged as AI. Small score leads can earn much larger shares of the round weight, which validators smooth across rounds.

Repeat predictions that differ after rounding to two decimal places or a running out-of-domain F1 below 0.90 zero the raw reward, and the FAQ warns that the baseline may miss that cutoff. Request attempts time out after 20 seconds by default, and rounds last at least 90 minutes. Port 70000 in the example is a placeholder, and the mining guide gives no registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/It-s-AI/llm-detection at commit f184874 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
