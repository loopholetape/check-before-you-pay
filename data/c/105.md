# Beam, Bittensor subnet 105: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Beam. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 24, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 105. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Decentralized bandwidth. A global network. Powering the open internet."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $0.36 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $0.11 and as high as $20.33, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 408 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 83 are still in the contest and 138 already have their fee back. Those entries paid a typical fee of $1.91, which is far from the $0.36 it costs now, so their record says little about an entry made at today's price. A typical one that is still in is paid $0.11 a day today, down from $1.38 a week ago, but most entries here do not last: only 83 of those 408 are still in, and an entry that is removed keeps what it was paid and loses its fee, so the days it would take to earn the fee back at that pay mean little.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 25 hours after an entry is made. $2,577 a day is paid to entrants today, 37 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 35% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Beam miner assigns data transfers to workers and reports which deliveries succeeded. On subnet 105, the miner needs a registered hotkey, which is the key a miner works with, along with a public gateway reachable by its workers and a TLS certificate and key. BeamCore returns an orchestrator ID and API key at registration, and workers need hotkeys but can join without buying subnet slots. The orchestrator setup requirements give no GPU, memory, CPU or disk minimum and list no mandatory paid service. The registration example sets the orchestrator’s fee to 10%.

Beam calculates confidence separately for standard and room transfers, with targets of 120 and 40 verified original tasks respectively. It compares the fraction of accountable tasks each miner completes successfully, and if both rates are zero, the miners draw. If positive rates tie and both completion times are available and finite, the faster miner wins unless the slower time is within 5% of the faster time, in which case they draw.

For pay, Beam ranks qualified miners by verified uploads. Each MiB counts at 1× for the top 30, then at 0.8×, 0.2× and 0.08× for successive groups of 30, and at 0.0075× thereafter. Fraud-report bonuses, sales, redistribution and penalties adjust those shares.

The code reads the number of blocks per epoch from the chain, and the orchestrator registration instructions give no registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/Beam-Network/beam at commit 79a536e on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
