# ms.computer, Bittensor subnet 24: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of ms.computer. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 22, 2026 at 08:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "The internet’s computer, run by everyone."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $18.59 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $0.12 and as high as $25.53, because it moves with demand. Here 50% of that price is held as collateral on the entry and is released only as the entry earns, while the rest is burned when the entry is made, so an entry that is never paid loses all of it. This page counts the whole price as the fee and counts collateral as coming back once it is released.

Of the 758 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 654 are still in the contest and 625 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid $0.97 a day today, up from $0.00 a week ago, so at today's pay the fee would take about 19 days to come back, provided the entry lasts that long.

## The contest today

1,015 of the 1,024 slots are taken, so a new entry does not remove anyone yet, and an entry cannot be removed for about 36 hours after it is made. $6,258 a day is paid to entrants today, 58 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 12% of the total.

## How the entries from two to four weeks ago ended up

351 entries from 51 wallets entered between Oct 26 and Nov 4, so each of them has had two full weeks, and they paid a typical fee of $22.31. After 7 days none of them were being paid $1 a day or more, and after 14 days 16 were. 135 of them, which is 38%, have by now been paid at least their fee, and the typical entry ended about even with its fee (-$0.15) before any cost of running it. By week of entry, oldest first, the share that got the fee back was 25% and 45%, so it has been rising from week to week.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. This page also cannot say what the contest asks of an entry, which hardware it needs or how it decides who is paid, and for that the contest's own code and rules have to be read. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
