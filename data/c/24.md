# ms.computer, Bittensor subnet 24: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of ms.computer. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 24, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 24. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "The internet’s computer, run by everyone."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $18.36 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $0.12 and as high as $25.53, because it moves with demand. Here 50% of that price is held as collateral on the entry and is released only as the entry earns, while the rest is burned when the entry is made, so an entry that is never paid loses all of it. This page counts the whole price as the fee and counts collateral as coming back once it is released.

Of the 980 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 674 are still in the contest and 843 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid $0.78 a day today, up from $0.00 a week ago, so at today's pay the fee would take about 24 days to come back, provided the entry lasts that long.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A miner on Ms. Computer, Bittensor subnet 24, hosts customers’ static websites, runs their web apps and can provide isolated sandboxes. For one sandbox, the installer expects an always-on x86_64 Ubuntu 24.04 server with at least four vCPUs, 15 GiB of RAM and 40 GiB free under /var/lib on a fresh host. The guide recommends 16 GiB of RAM and about 100 GiB free. It specifies a static public IP and inbound TCP ports 8091 and 8094 but gives no GPU requirement.

The coldkey, which holds the funds, stays off the server. The miner signs its address enrollment with its registered hotkey, the key it works with, and uses a TLS certificate and private key for incoming connections. Its two local processes share a bridge secret. The agent generates a service key and uses it to sign requests for workload artifacts while the hotkey is registered and the service binding is live. Registration costs TAO, and endpoint enrollment requires no chain transaction.

Validators, the participants who score miners’ work, check the published software, the resources the miner claims and whether it serves work correctly. Qualification takes about four hours once the miner is enrolled and serving, and an individual challenge allows up to five minutes.

The installer renews enrollment about every six hours because it expires after 24 hours. Restarting the agent ends all active sandboxes. Read from https://github.com/misscomputer/mscomputer-miner at commit eb1b863 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
