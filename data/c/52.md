# Dojo, Bittensor subnet 52: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Dojo. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 27, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 52. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $189 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $181 and as high as $257, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Nobody entered between 2 and 14 days ago, so there is nothing to say about what a new entry gets right now.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 36 hours after an entry is made. Nobody outside the contest's owner is being paid today.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

In an active round, a Dojo miner either answers a prompt or votes for the better of two answers. To participate in subnet 52, miners connect their registered Bittensor hotkey, the key a miner works with, through a browser wallet such as Talisman. They need no server, and the README’s miner instructions list no GPU, GPU memory, processor or storage minimum, public port, third-party account, API key or paid service.

In a duel between miners, the authors split one point according to their votes, and the voters share another equally. When a miner’s answer goes up against an answer from a validator, a participant that scores the miners’ work, each vote carries an equal share of one point. That share goes to the voter for choosing the validator or to the author for winning that vote. The README tells registered miners to wait for tasks, but the validator at this commit skips new rounds and assigns 100% of its reward weight to burn UID 158, leaving 0% for other miners.

Choosing the inferior answer in a trap costs 0.4 points in code, although the scoring function’s comment says 1. On a scored task with at least one vote, active voters who were assigned to vote and have no score entry share a 4-point penalty equally. Production rounds are scheduled three hours apart, and code-generation tasks are given six hours to expiry at creation. The README’s miner instructions give no registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/tensorplex-labs/dojo at commit 283d039 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
