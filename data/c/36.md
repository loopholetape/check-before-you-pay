# Weir, Bittensor subnet 36: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Weir. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 28, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 36. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "GPU compute marketplace. Rent full GPU machines with root SSH."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $54.95 right now, and over the last 26 days it has been as low as $0.12 and as high as $79.15, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Nobody entered between 2 and 14 days ago, so there is nothing to say about what a new entry gets right now.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Weir miner rents out GPUs by the hour and runs an agent that answers timed checks and starts a Docker container for each rental. The Ubuntu host needs an eligible NVIDIA card and reachable SSH ports. The miner registers a hotkey, which is the key a miner works with, and gives Weir support the control address privately. Each GPU carries a nonrefundable activation fee, and a pre-registration quote obtained with a Weir API key holds for seven days if it names a hotkey.

To qualify for idle pay, the miner needs 14 consecutive days of successful checks, Sysbox, disk space of at least 1.5 times total GPU memory, a price within 50–100% of the card’s cap and replies to at least half the checks. Rentals lasting at least ten minutes share 10% of miner emissions by weighted GPU-hours, and each miner’s share is capped at the platform fees its rentals generated. Idle cards receive up to 90%, and the network fund receives the balance. Rent becomes payable 48 hours after the rental ends, less refunds and Weir’s 5% fee, and payouts are made weekly in USDT or USDC once $5 is due.

If the machine fails a renter, that rental earns nothing and the miner loses 48 hours of its rented-pool share. The miner README gives no post-registration protection period or current weight-setting round length. Read from https://github.com/weircompute/weir-subnet at commit b3823a1 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several. A new contest took over this subnet number on Nov 3, and nothing before that day is counted.
