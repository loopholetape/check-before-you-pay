# MVTRX, Bittensor subnet 79: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of MVTRX. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 24, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 79. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A miner on MVTRX places and cancels orders in simulated markets and can also train a shared model. Trading needs a registered Bittensor hotkey, which is the key a miner works with, and a reachable port, normally 8091. The project estimates four CPU cores, 4 GB of RAM and 100 GB of free disk. Training allows about four minutes of work per five-minute round, and a CPU can miss that deadline, so the setup guide recommends an NVIDIA GPU with at least 8 GB of memory. Training also needs a storage bucket with private write credentials and public read credentials, and storage and request charges depend on the provider.

Trading receives at least 95% of miner rewards by default, and half the trading pool pays miners in proportion to the spread they keep on buy and sell orders after 20 simulated seconds. The other half rewards profit beyond market movements across enough order books, with a rank-based payout if nobody qualifies. Training shares up to 5% according to how much model updates improve predictions on unseen data.

Validators, the participants that score the miners' work, normally allow three seconds to reply, and scoring starts in the first cycle despite the stated 24-hour registration protection. If you hit the volume cap, you can only cancel orders on the affected book. Read from https://github.com/taos-im/sn-79 at commit d717c50 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
