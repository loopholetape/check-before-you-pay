# Witness, Bittensor subnet 20: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Witness. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 26, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 20. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Decentralized models to understand video"

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $36.52 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $36.13 and as high as $51.45, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Nobody entered between 2 and 14 days ago, so there is nothing to say about what a new entry gets right now.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Witness miner registers a hotkey, the key a miner works with, and serves a fixed video model so validators, the participants who score the miners’ work, can download and run it. You need a CPU server with a public port, normally 8091, that stays reachable during evaluation. The server needs room for the model, which is capped at 24 or 44 GB depending on architecture, but serving requires no GPU. The miner guide specifies no third-party API account for serving the model, while evaluators need API credentials for labeling and judging. Chain transactions can cost money, although Witness charges no challenge fee.

Each evaluator tests contenders on the same clips and compares their timestamped descriptions of sights, speech, text and sounds against two references, rewarding accuracy, coverage and timing. From window 30, answer speed contributes up to 10% of the clip’s quality score to its reward. To replace the winner, a challenger needs quality above 0.05 and the largest reward lead above 0.02 after weighting by evaluator stake. The winner gets all normal miner weight, while all weight burns without a registered winner or under an operator override.

Registration and submission must both happen before the opening block of a two-epoch window. From window 13, the code allows up to 30 minutes per paired attempt, including preparation, with 60 seconds per clip. Each evaluator allows 15 minutes per model for active transfer and verification across retries and windows, excluding time in the queue. The cited guides give no duration for protection from deregistration. Each hotkey binds one model apart from a one-time reset at window 32. The 100,000-alpha download threshold applies to validators, who can keep or redistribute the weights. Read from https://github.com/witnessvision/witness_subnet/ at commit 4770712 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
