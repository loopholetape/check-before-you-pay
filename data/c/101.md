# Tag101, Bittensor subnet 101: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Tag101. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 28, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 101. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Tag101 is a Bittensor subnet for decentralized social post tagging"

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $4.25 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $1.65 and as high as $24.02, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 716 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 46 are still in the contest and 455 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid $6.28 a day today, about the same as a week ago, when it was $6.72, but most entries here do not last: only 46 of those 716 are still in, and an entry that is removed keeps what it was paid and loses its fee, so the days it would take to earn the fee back at that pay mean little.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 24 hours after an entry is made. $1,642 a day is paid to entrants today, 22 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 56% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Tag101 miner labels an X post with up to three tags that name its subjects, people or events. The README describes a database API, but the code for the validator, a participant that scores the miners’ work, picks a random post from the corpus. Rounds normally start every 15 minutes and give miners 120 seconds to reply.

The miner needs a registered wallet hotkey, the key a miner works with, and a server with a reachable port, two 2 GHz x86-64 CPU cores, 8 GB of RAM, 4 GB of swap and 20 GB free. No GPU is required. For a nonblank post, the reference miner calls OpenAI with an API key at an unstated price, but if it has no key, it returns one random crypto tag. If the text is blank, it returns no tags.

Validators give equal weight to agreement with other miners, relevance and label quality. Only the best tag per qualifying group counts, and the code divides by three even if fewer tags arrive. If 50 miners submit the same set, each keeps half its score. Observed miners share weight in proportion to the exponential of ten times their 24-hour average, so missing observations count as zeros and higher averages earn larger shares. The burn share is zero.

The cited settings give no registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/tag101-ai/tag101 at commit c3d788b on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
