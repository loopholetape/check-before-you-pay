# Yugos, Bittensor subnet 34: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Yugos. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 28, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 34. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "System one decision models for inference"

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $91.59 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $90.32 and as high as $141, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 11 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, all 11 are still in the contest and none has its fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today, so at today's pay the fee does not come back.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 17 hours after an entry is made. $6,280 a day is paid to entrants today, 2 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 85% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A Yugos miner trains a router to choose a provider, model and reasoning effort for each turn, then uploads it with a hotkey, the key a miner works with, registered on subnet 34. The guide specifies no miner hardware minimum, public port or always-on server. Yugos runs the router and grades the answers, so hosted evaluation needs no provider account or API key, while local tests use the miner’s keys and credit. A new v0.2.0 evaluation reserves $300 for model calls, compute and evaluation, then releases the unused balance.

Challengers face the champion’s questions across chat, math and code, and agent tasks. Hard answers count double, while extra spending and working time lower the score. Every category must pass its checks, and the statistical lower bound on the challenger’s advantage must exceed 0.2 points. The current champion receives 85% and the two preceding champions receive 10% and 5% respectively, with any vacant shares going to the current champion.

Canceling the upload leaves a funded run active. The job deadline is 86,000 seconds, and new uploads are limited over a rolling 24 hours. The guide gives no fixed round length or registration protection period, and the starter’s fixed choice must be replaced with learned routing. Read from https://github.com/BitMind-AI/yugos at commit 1644773 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
