This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Bitcast. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 23, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 93. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A miner on Bitcast’s YouTube mechanism publishes public videos that meet a brand’s brief and keeps a server reachable on a public IP address, using port 8091 by default. The miner works with a registered hotkey, the key a miner works with, and connects a YouTube Partner Program channel through OAuth credentials or Bitcast’s keyed token service. The channel must be at least 21 days old, have 100 to 500,000 subscribers, and reach 1,000 minutes watched and 10% average percentage viewed over 90 days. The cited passages give no hardware minimums or additional miner fees.

About every four hours, validators, the participants who score the miners’ work, check how well videos fit the brief and use seven-day averages to score growth in YouTube Premium revenue. The 14-day scoring window and three-day analytics delay give a 17-day age cutoff. Dollar scores determine reward shares after caps and smoothing, and unused rewards go to burn UID 0. The README assigns roughly 2% to YouTube and 98% to X, while the code reads the chain split and uses 85% if that lookup yields unusable data.

Creators can publish from three days before a brief starts through its final day, but restricted briefs pay only listed channels, and videos flagged as having captions fail screening. The cited passages give no registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/bitcast-network/bitcast at commit b8af435 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
