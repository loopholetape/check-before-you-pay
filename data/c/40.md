# Ralph, Bittensor subnet 40: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Ralph. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 28, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 40. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "The same model, small enough to run on your phone. Compress the frontier models into GGUFs."

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A miner compresses Qwen3-8B into GGUF or safetensors for one of four size tiers while keeping its architecture and weight count. The miner uses a registered Bittensor hotkey, the key a miner works with, to commit a salted hash of the exact files that validators, the participants who score the miners’ work, will fetch from a fixed Hugging Face revision. The miner guide lists no minimum miner hardware or storage, public port, always-on server, paid account or separate judge API key, and the resubmission bond is zero.

Validators run GPU scoring, and three judges compare how the compressed and original models’ next steps change each judge’s predictions about the same continuation. Ralph averages the three scores, using each judge’s weakest result across language and reasoning-depth groups. Binary, ternary, sub2 and sub4 carry weights of 40%, 25%, 20% and 15% across occupied tiers, and those weights are normalized if any tiers are empty. A champion keeps its tier’s full share unless another miner’s best-challenging model earns 20% with a strictly positive paired lower confidence bound. Burn records direct everything to one recipient, and an allocation takes effect on chain when validators apply it.

Each coldkey, the key that holds the funds, gets one entry per tier per round, and losing the saved salt prevents a reveal. The guide says to reveal after the round opens, but the code skips entries whose latest write, including the reveal, has a known block height at or after the block that draws the exam. The lower bound of the 100-block lookback is advisory, and the miner guide gives no fixed round length or registration protection period. Read from https://github.com/RalphLabsAI/ralph at commit 9304344 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
