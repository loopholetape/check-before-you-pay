# blockmachine, Bittensor subnet 19: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of blockmachine. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 27, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 19. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Harnessing Bittensor's incentive layer to forge self-optimizing infrastructure."

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $189 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $181 and as high as $257, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 13 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, all 13 are still in the contest and 4 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid $53.07 a day today, up from $36.31 a week ago, so at today's pay the fee would take about 4 days to come back, provided the entry lasts that long.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 3 days after an entry is made. $4,223 a day is paid to entrants today, 29 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 27% of the total.

## How the entries from two to four weeks ago ended up

9 entries from 9 wallets entered between Oct 29 and Nov 13, so each of them has had two full weeks, and they paid a typical fee of $226. After 7 days 5 of them were being paid $1 a day or more, and after 14 days 2 were. 5 of them, which is 56%, have by now been paid at least their fee, and the typical entry ended $397 ahead of its fee before any cost of running it. A group of 9 is small, so these shares can swing a long way on a few entries.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. This page also cannot say what the contest asks of an entry, which hardware it needs or how it decides who is paid, and for that the contest's own code and rules have to be read. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
