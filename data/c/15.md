# ORO, Bittensor subnet 15: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of ORO. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 24, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 15. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "AI commerce agents"

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $35.01 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $19.51 and as high as $96.59, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 712 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, 123 are still in the contest and 29 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

## The contest today

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 17 hours after an entry is made. $8,582 a day is paid to entrants today, 36 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 96% of the total.

## How the entries from two to four weeks ago ended up

913 entries from 221 wallets entered between Oct 25 and Nov 10, so each of them has had two full weeks, and they paid a typical fee of $33.94. After 7 days 18 of them were being paid $1 a day or more, and after 14 days none were. 56 of them, which is 6%, have by now been paid at least their fee, and the typical entry ended $27.58 behind its fee before any cost of running it. By week of entry, oldest first, the share that got the fee back was 2%, 8% and 5%.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

Miners on ORO submit Python agents that shop for simulated customers, ask about missing requirements when the world provides a messaging tool, and place one order or decline. Validators, who are participants that score the miners' work, run the submitted agents in Docker, so miners need no always-on server or public port for evaluation. Local Docker testing needs at least 16 GB of free disk for the first image downloads. The miner guide lists an OpenRouter key for local testing, and the local code also accepts Chutes.

A task earns one point for a fully correct finish within budget and zero otherwise, and a challenger must exceed a decaying score threshold to take the lead. The designated leader receives the top emission weight, while other survivors of the latest completed race receive progressively smaller weights by race rank. The baseline burn rate is configurable and defaults to 75% when the public response omits the emission policy.

By default, validators allow five minutes per task and 30 minutes for the sandbox run. The validator code mentions that registration protection expires, but that passage does not specify its length. Read from https://github.com/ORO-AI/oro at commit 9eba926 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
