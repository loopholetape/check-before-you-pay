# Teutonic, Bittensor subnet 3: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of Teutonic. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 26, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 3. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

## If you are about to pay the entry fee

The fee is $183 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $183 and as high as $369, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

Of the 65 entries made 2 to 14 days ago, all 65 are still in the contest and 37 already have their fee back. A typical one that is still in is paid nothing today even so, which means that pay here comes in bursts or goes to very few entries at a time.

All 256 slots are taken, so every new entry removes the lowest-paid entry that is past its protected period, which lasts about 33 hours after an entry is made. $8,910 a day is paid to entrants today, 3 wallets receive $5 a day or more, and the largest wallet takes 40% of the total.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A miner trains Teutonic’s 110-billion-parameter language model and uploads a complete checkpoint to challenge one competition’s leader on subnet 3. The guide describes training with eight H200- or B200-class GPUs or better, or with a multi-node setup, and the weights alone occupy about 220 GB in BF16. GPU rental is optional, and the upload cap is 250 GB. The validator, a participant that scores the miners’ work, supplies upload credentials through an Ed25519 hotkey, the key a miner works with. The credentials last seven days and renew a day before expiry while eligible.

The validator evaluates the models remotely. Both models predict the same text, and the code weights reductions in loss above Horizon by bytes, though the guide uses token-based wording. For the documented rollout, the lower confidence bound must exceed 0.0005 bits per byte. Under the fixed policy preceding the rollout, MAIN gets 40%, while MIX01, MIX06 and MIX07 get 20% each. After activation, MAIN2 takes 40% and MIX07 keeps 20%, while the first winners of MIX08 and MIX09 each take the oldest remaining retired leader’s share.

The repository describes the rollout as prepared, and live activation was unverified. The submission code defaults to MAIN2, though the guide’s submission instructions name MAIN. Finalizing the ready signal permanently spends that hotkey’s one entry, even if the checkpoint is rejected. The miner guide gives no registration protection period, submission deadline or fixed round length. Read from https://github.com/unarbos/teutonic at commit 31924e3 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
