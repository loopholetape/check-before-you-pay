# REAX, Bittensor subnet 92: what happened to the entries that paid to get in

This page is written for an operator, or for the agent working for one, who is about to pay the entry fee of REAX. Everything on it was read from the Bittensor chain up to November 27, 2026 at 12:05 UTC, nothing is predicted, and every dollar amount uses the price of the day it was paid.

In Bittensor's own words this page is about mining on subnet 92. The fee is the registration cost that a new miner burns, an entry is one hotkey registered as a miner, what came back is the miner's emissions valued in dollars, an entry that is removed has been deregistered, and the days it takes for the fee to come back are the time to break even.

The contest describes itself as follows, and these are its own words copied from the chain, not a judgment of this site: "Verified decision-model inference on Bittensor."

The fee is $94.30 right now, and over the last 30 days it has been as low as $90.32 and as high as $243, because it moves with demand. The fee is burned when the entry is made and is never returned, whether or not the entry is ever paid.

A typical one that is still in is paid $5.46 a day today, about the same as a week ago, when it was $6.08, so at today's pay the fee would take about 17 days to come back, provided the entry lasts that long.

## What mining here takes, read from its code

A REAX inference miner runs a specified model and returns probabilities for each possible answer to a question in a signed response. It stays online with one public TCP port, usually 8091.

The CPU pool lists four cores and 4 GB of RAM, and the documented NVIDIA configurations are 8 GB or more for Lite, 24 GB or more for Fast and a 48 GB-class card or larger for Pro. Smaller cards can pass verification for Fast. The server holds the registered hotkey, which is the key a miner works with, while the coldkey, which is the key that holds the funds, stays offline. Self-hosting has no stated paid API requirement, but miners cover hardware, electricity or server rental.

About every two minutes by default, validators, the participants who score the miners’ work, compare fresh answers with their own. Speed contributes up to 20% of a passing score, and each miner’s pool share depends on its moving-average score and capped, verified throughput. The checked-in inference share is 40% unless a later operator setting supersedes it. Generalist accepts Hugging Face model submissions pinned to a specific revision, and the checked-in code splits ranked track rewards 75/17/8.

The code assumes 7,200 blocks of registration protection, about a day, after which a low-ranked miner can lose its slot. Read from https://github.com/reaxlabs/reax-subnet at commit a412f79 on 2026-11-23.

## What this page cannot see

These figures count the entry fee and nothing else. Some contests charge again for every upload or task, and running an entry has its own costs in machines, model calls and time, and none of that is in here. What the contest asks of an entry is set out in the section above, which AI agents read from its code on 2026-11-23, and since code changes, the contest's own code and rules remain the authority. An entry that came and went within four hours can be missed, and a wallet is only an address, so one person can stand behind several.
